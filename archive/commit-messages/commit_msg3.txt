The paper's group size does not rescue the paper's learning rate

G=32 + lr 5e-5, the paper's own setting, is now measured. It does not recover:
macro-F1 0.5403 against SFT's 0.6114, CI [0.4807, 0.5965] overlapping SFT's
[0.5496, 0.6602]. Group size does help -- holding the lr at 5e-5, going from 8
to 32 moves the score 0.5014 to 0.5403. But that is +0.039, against the +0.108
that dropping the lr from 5e-5 to 1e-5 buys at G=8. The learning rate dominates
by roughly 2.8x, and the paper's learning rate is not usable at this scale.

Two per-class collapses say more about the mechanism than the scores do. At lr
5e-5 each run loses an entire class, and not the same one: G=8 drops `none` to
0.000 F1, G=32 drops `Donut` to 0.000. Both recover at lr 1e-5. A whole class
vanishing is not the stable bias a hard class would produce -- it moves when the
group size moves, which is what a policy pushed into a degenerate region looks
like.

G=32 reshapes the error profile rather than scaling it. It is best of all runs
on radial_zone (0.4603 vs SFT 0.2640), best on retrieval (mAP@10 0.4337 vs SFT
0.3873, so the run that scores worst on the headline is the best at retrieval),
best on clock MAE and on Scratch -- and worst on size MAE (1.6509, 3.3x SFT) and
caption. "It got worse" is the wrong summary.

Its idle-step share is 42.00% (63/150), the highest of the four RL runs, while
its macro-F1 beats the G=8 run whose idle share is 38.00%. That is a third
independent case of the signal and the score moving apart.
